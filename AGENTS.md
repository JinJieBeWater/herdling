## Lessons

- When unifying panel surfaces, compare corner curvature relative to control height across navigation, source headers, ordinary rows and edge rows. Shell clearance is not a control radius: keep internal corners in the rounded-control family and adapt only corners meeting the shell. Measure visible background-to-shell clearance along straight edges and turns; shared columns or radius centres alone do not establish consistency. Preserve approved footer geometry unless the user authorizes replacing it; separate fills from hit bounds and align glyph/title anchors with role-specific optical budgets.

- When integrating an overlay scrollbar, compare hidden, scrolling, hover and drag states across the whole panel. Check glyphs, surfaces and hit areas together; a permanent one-sided lane fixes overlap but makes ordinary rows lopsided. Design content boundaries and indicator placement together rather than shaving row backgrounds locally.

- When matching Tinycast icon language, inspect its list symbols and header controls separately, then match weight, base variant and tint by role. Use the reference rather than assuming Herdling's existing inner status glyph is already the right baseline; keep transparent slots and state meaning.

- When changing the source accordion, allow all sources to collapse and keep at most one expanded. Clicking or pressing Enter on the current source collapses it; another source replaces it. Keep mouse and keyboard paths consistent, preserve saved expansion, and never navigate terminals from a source header.

- When presenting routine working/idle state, let the leading status glyph carry it; keep trailing text for attention, pending or unavailable feedback. Style navigation choices as controls, not section titles; preserve equal type metrics across selection changes.

- When refining roster controls, compare default, hover, current, pressed, keyboard and disabled states across the whole panel first. Share state meaning and priority, but size optical spacing for each control family instead of copying the list's glyph slot into short buttons. Activity includes working and idle; Sources browses directories. Keep bare role glyphs, explicit offline copy, direct switching and stable type metrics; the current light-navigation contract keeps inactive hover unfilled until a whole-panel replacement is approved.

- When changing navigation, compare the existing client reuse and launch path before replacing its protocol. Verify the user's visible client and destination, not just server focus. Keep temporary sessions out of the user's live roster during tests.

- When removing panel chrome or captions, recheck both exposed list edges and the fixed-header/list gap. Keep a quiet one-line empty state and reuse the existing view switch instead of adding counters or duplicate actions. Edge rounding follows the actual content boundary; ordinary row corners stay compact.

- When adding a header control or trailing readout, measure its background edge and content slot against roster rows. Avoid doubled insets and empty trailing views that still consume stack spacing; top hover fills must follow the same content contour as bottom rows.

- When changing panel chrome, keep the panel roster-only: Refresh and Settings belong in the status item's context menu, not an independent footer. Judge changes in the whole panel; matching fonts and alignment alone does not establish cohesion.
